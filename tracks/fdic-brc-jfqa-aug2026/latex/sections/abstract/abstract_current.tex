I provide causal evidence that uninsured depositors discipline community
banks in response to mandatory credit-risk disclosure.  I exploit the
Current Expected Credit Loss (CECL) Day-One retained-earnings
adjustment, an information shock whose magnitude was fixed by past
underwriting.  Banks with large disclosed adjustments lost 0.39
percentage points of assets in uninsured time deposits after adoption,
about 7 percent of the sample mean, and gained a similar amount of
insured time deposits.  The timing separates the two actors: banks,
which knew their charges privately, began substituting into insured
brokered and reciprocal deposits and electing regulatory capital relief
quarters before adoption, while depositor-facing outcomes moved only
after the disclosure became public.  Discipline operates as quantity
exit at the rollover margin.  Banks replaced the withdrawn uninsured
funding with insured wholesale deposits, and their interest expense
rose while profitability fell.
