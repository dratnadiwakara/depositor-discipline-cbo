We provide causal evidence that uninsured depositors discipline community banks
in response to mandatory credit-risk disclosures.  Exploiting the Current
Expected Credit Loss (CECL) Day-One retained-earnings adjustment as an
information shock whose magnitude was fixed by past underwriting, we show that
within the same bank and quarter, uninsured time deposits fell relative to
insured deposits in proportion to the disclosed adjustment---an estimate robust
to allowing every 2023-stress exposure characteristic its own time path.
High-adjustment banks replaced the lost funding with insured brokered and
reciprocal deposits at higher cost, suffered lower profitability, and slowed
lending; part of the withdrawn money reappears at local competitors.  Effects
on pricing are amplified where depositors are financially sophisticated.
