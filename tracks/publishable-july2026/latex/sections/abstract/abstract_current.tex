We provide causal evidence that uninsured depositors discipline community
banks in response to mandatory credit-risk disclosures.  Exploiting the
Current Expected Credit Loss (CECL) Day-One retained-earnings adjustment
as an information shock whose magnitude was fixed by past underwriting,
we show that uninsured time deposits fell relative to insured deposits
in proportion to the disclosed adjustment.  High-adjustment banks
replaced the lost funding with insured brokered and reciprocal deposits
at higher cost, suffered lower profitability, and slowed lending.
Pricing effects are amplified where depositors are financially
sophisticated.
